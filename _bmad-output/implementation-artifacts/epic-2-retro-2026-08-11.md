## What Went Well

- **Zero architecture-spine amendments this epic** — a first. AD-4 (status transition ownership), AD-7 (keyed-DI order processors), AD-10 (concurrency), and AD-11 (single pricing-strategy registration) all held exactly as written against real implementation, unlike Epic 1's three mid-review amendments.
- **A previous epic's convention paid off directly.** Story 2.4's `ConcurrencyConflictException` gap in `TransitionTo` was fixed by reusing the exact try/catch → `Result.Failure` pattern Epic 1's `ProductService.UpdateAsync` had already established — not reinvented, just applied.
- **Every real bug this epic was caught before merge**, not discovered later — no regressions reported once fixed.

## Growth Areas

- **Patch count is a weak severity proxy — the epic's clearest lesson.** Story 2.5 had only 2 review patches, the fewest of any story this epic, and they were the two most serious correctness bugs in the project to date:
  1. Duplicate line items for the same product were stock-checked *independently* rather than by combined quantity — an order could persist despite total demand exceeding stock, violating the "no partial decrement" guarantee.
  2. The inventory-decrement step blindly subtracted stock with no re-validation, so two near-simultaneous order confirmations on the same product could drive stock negative without tripping the `RowVersion` concurrency check (which only catches stale-read-then-write races, not fresh-read-then-oversell sequences).

   Both were fixed (aggregate-by-`ProductId` before the stock check; guard-then-subtract instead of blind-subtract, with regression tests added for both). If patch count alone had been used as a quality signal, Story 2.5 would have looked like the safest story in the epic — it was the riskiest one caught.
- Story 2.5 deferred 6 items (Standard/Rush processor orchestration duplication, N+1 stock-check round-trips, no total shown in the UI before/after confirmation, no client-side stock feedback, silent no-op on Remove Item with nothing selected, quantity not reset after Add Item) — all disclosed UX/performance polish, none required by any AC, consistent with this epic's otherwise low defer counts (2/1/0/0).
